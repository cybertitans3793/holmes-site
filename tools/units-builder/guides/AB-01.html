Unit 1 · Kinematics

How to use this: read the key ideas, then cover each worked example and try it yourself before reading the solution. Do the self-check questions on paper before you open the answers, mark the ones you miss, and come back to them in a day or two without looking. Rereading won't do much; answering questions will.

Key ideas

  • Distance is how much ground you cover. It is a scalar: just a number, no direction.
  • Displacement is how far out of place you end up: final position minus starting position. It is a vector: a number and a direction.
  • If you never turn around, distance and displacement have the same size. A round trip always has zero displacement.
  • Speed is how fast you cover ground. Velocity is speed with a direction.
  • Average speed uses distance. Average velocity uses displacement. Both divide by the total time.
  • An object is accelerating when its velocity changes. There are three ways: speed up, slow down, or turn.
  • Acceleration is the change in velocity each second. An acceleration of 5 m/s/s means the velocity changes by 5 m/s every second.
  • Direction of acceleration: speeding up, it points the way the object moves. Slowing down, it points the opposite way.
  • A minus sign tells you a direction (left, west, down). It does not mean “less than nothing,” and a negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.
  • Position–time graphs: slope = velocity. Straight line: constant velocity. Flat line: at rest. Curved line: the velocity is changing (steeper and steeper means speeding up).
  • Velocity–time graphs: above the axis means moving + ; below means moving −. Slope = acceleration. Area between the line and the axis = displacement.
  • Free fall: only gravity acts. Everything falls with the same acceleration, 9.8 m/s/s down (we use about 10), whatever its mass.
  • In free fall the velocity changes by −10 m/s each second, going up and coming down. At the top, v = 0 for an instant, but the acceleration is still 10 m/s/s down.

Units: distance and displacement in m, time in s, velocity in m/s, acceleration in m/s/s.

Common mistakes

  • Using distance to find average velocity. Average velocity needs displacement.
  • Thinking negative acceleration always means slowing down. Moving in the − direction and speeding up also gives a negative acceleration.
  • Reading a velocity–time graph like a position–time graph. On a v–t graph a flat line means constant velocity, not stopped. Stopped is a flat line on the axis.
  • Saying the acceleration is zero at the top of a throw. Only the velocity is zero there.
  • Thinking heavier things fall faster. Without air resistance, they fall together.
  • Forgetting that area below the time axis counts as displacement in the − direction.

How to solve a back-and-forth trip

  1. Draw a number line. Mark the start, each turn-around point and the finish.
  2. Distance: find the length of each leg (no signs) and add them up.
  3. Displacement: final position − starting position. Give the direction (+ or −, east or west).
  4. Find the total time for the whole trip.
  5. Average speed = distance ÷ time. Average velocity = displacement ÷ time, with a direction.
  6. Check: average speed can never be smaller than the size of the average velocity.

Finding an acceleration

  • Pick a + direction. Write vi, vf and t with signs.
  • Find Δv = vf − vi. Keep the sign.
  • Divide: a = Δv ÷ t. The sign of a is its direction.
  • Check with the rule: speeding up → a points the way it moves. Slowing down → a points the other way.
  • From a data table: find how much the velocity changes each second. That change is a.

Reading a graph

  • First, read the axis label. Position–time and velocity–time graphs follow different rules.
  • Split the graph into stages wherever the line changes.
  • For each stage, pick two points on the line. Slope = rise ÷ run.
  • Position–time: slope is the velocity. Velocity–time: slope is the acceleration.
  • Velocity–time displacement: cut the area into rectangles and triangles, find each one, then add.

Free fall

  • Up is +. The velocity drops by 10 m/s every second.
  • Count down by tens until you reach 0. That is the top.
  • Keep counting into negatives for the trip down. Same height means same speed, opposite direction.
  • “Dropped” means it starts at 0 m/s.
Distance and displacement

A delivery robot in a hallway

A robot rolls along a hallway marked in meters. It starts at the 4 m mark, rolls to 22 m, back to 1 m, then forward to 13 m. The whole trip takes 25 s. Find its distance, displacement, average speed and average velocity.

Distance: add the legs, ignoring direction.

d=18+21+12 d=51m

Displacement: final position minus starting position.

Δx=13−4 Δx=9m, forward (+)

Average speed uses distance:

avg speed=dt=5125 avg speed≈2.0m/s

Average velocity uses displacement:

avg velocity=Δxt=925 avg velocity=0.36m/s, forward

The robot covered a lot of ground but ended up only 9 m from where it began. Turning around makes the displacement (and the average velocity) much smaller than the distance (and the average speed).

Acceleration and its sign

A cyclist brakes while riding west

A cyclist rides west at 12 m/s. She brakes and slows to 3 m/s in 4.5 s. Call east +. Find her acceleration (size and direction).

Moving west means negative velocities:

vi=−12m/svf=−3m/s Δv=vf−vi=−3−(−12) Δv=+9m/s a=Δvt=+94.5 a=+2.0m/s/s, east

The acceleration is positive but she is slowing down. That fits the rule: she moves west and slows down, so the acceleration points the other way, east. The sign tells you a direction, not “speeding up.”

Position–time graph

A toy car's three-stage trip

The position–time graph shows a toy car. Find its velocity in each stage and describe what it is doing. Then find its total distance and its displacement.

Stage A (0–4 s): slope = rise ÷ run.

vA=12−04−0 vA=+3m/s

Stage B (4–6 s): the line is flat.

vB=0m/s (at rest)

Stage C (6–10 s):

vC=4−1210−6=−84 vC=−2m/s

Distance: 12 m out, then 8 m back.

d=12+8=20m

Displacement: final position − starting position.

Δx=4−0=+4m

A: moves + at a steady 3 m/s. B: stopped. C: moves back (−) at a steady 2 m/s, slower than in A because the line is less steep. Straight lines mean constant velocity in every stage.

Velocity–time graph

A scooter speeds up, cruises and stops

The velocity–time graph shows a scooter. Find the acceleration in each stage and the total displacement.

Accelerations are slopes:

a1=10−45−0=1.2m/s/s a2=0m/s/s(flat line) a3=0−1014−9=−2m/s/s

Displacement is the area. Stage 1 is a rectangle under a triangle:

A1=(5)(4)+12(5)(6)=20+15=35m

Stage 2 is a rectangle, 4 s wide:

A2=(4)(10)=40m

Stage 3 is a triangle, 5 s wide:

A3=12(5)(10)=25m Δx=35+40+25=100m

Stage 1: the line moves away from the axis, so it speeds up. Stage 3: it moves toward the axis, so it slows down, and the slope is negative. The whole graph stays above the axis, so the scooter always moves in the + direction.

Free fall

A ball tossed straight up

You toss a ball straight up at 25 m/s. Ignore air resistance and use g = 10 m/s/s. Fill in its velocity each second. When does it reach the top? How long is it in the air? How high does it go?

Up is +. Subtract 10 m/s every second:

0 s: +25 · 1 s: +15 · 2 s: +5 · 3 s: −5 · 4 s: −15 · 5 s: −25 (m/s)

Time to the top (where v = 0):

tup=vi10=2510 tup=2.5s

Time down = time up:

tair=2.5+2.5=5.0s

Height = shaded area on the v–t graph (a triangle):

height=12(2.5)(25) height≈31m

At 2 s it moves up at 5 m/s; at 3 s it moves down at 5 m/s, at the same height. The acceleration is 10 m/s/s down the whole time, even at the top where v = 0.

Self-check

Answer first, then open.

  1. A dog runs 30 m out to fetch a ball and trots back to drop it at your feet. It does this 3 times. What are its distance and its displacement?
    Answer
    Distance = 3 × 60 = 180 m. Displacement = 0 m, because it ended where it started (a round trip).
  2. A bus travels 18 km in 0.25 hours. What is its average speed?
    Answer
    avg speed = d ÷ t = 18 ÷ 0.25 = 72 km/h.
  3. When is an object's average speed bigger than the size of its average velocity?
    Answer
    When it changes direction. Turning back adds to the distance but takes away from the displacement. With no turn-around, the two are the same size.
  4. A car speeds up from 6 m/s to 20 m/s in 3.5 s. Find its acceleration.
    Answer
    Δv = 20 − 6 = 14 m/s. a = 14 ÷ 3.5 = 4.0 m/s/s, in the direction it moves.
  5. A cart moves to the left (the − direction) and speeds up. Is its acceleration + or −? Why?
    Answer
    Negative. It is speeding up, so the acceleration points the way it moves, which is left (−). A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.
  6. A velocity table reads: 0 s, 18 m/s; 1 s, 15 m/s; 2 s, 12 m/s; 3 s, 9 m/s. Describe the motion and find the acceleration.
    Answer
    Moving in the + direction and slowing down at a steady rate. The velocity drops 3 m/s each second, so a = −3 m/s/s.
  7. On a position–time graph, the line curves and gets steeper and steeper. What is the object doing?
    Answer
    Speeding up. The slope is the velocity, and the slope keeps getting bigger.
  8. A velocity–time graph is a flat line at −6 m/s from 0 s to 5 s. Describe the motion and find the displacement.
    Answer
    Constant velocity of 6 m/s in the − direction (not stopped). Area = (5)(−6) = −30 m, so the displacement is 30 m in the − direction.
  9. A velocity–time line goes straight from +10 m/s at 0 s to −10 m/s at 4 s. What happens at 2 s? What is the acceleration? What is the displacement from 0 to 4 s?
    Answer
    At 2 s the line crosses the axis: the object stops for an instant and turns around. a = (−10 − 10) ÷ 4 = −5 m/s/s. The area above the axis (+10 m) and the area below (−10 m) cancel, so the displacement is 0 m: it is back where it started.
  10. A rock is dropped off a cliff. How fast is it moving after 3 s? What is its acceleration then? (Ignore air resistance.)
    Answer
    It gains 10 m/s each second from 0, so after 3 s it moves at 30 m/s downward. The acceleration is still 10 m/s/s downward, the same as at every other moment.
  11. A ball thrown straight up lands back in the thrower's hand 7.0 s later. About how fast was it thrown?
    Answer
    Time up = time down, so it took 3.5 s to reach the top. It loses 10 m/s each second, so it started at about 3.5 × 10 = 35 m/s.
  12. A bowling ball and a marble are dropped together from the same height. Ignoring air resistance, which lands first?
    Answer
    They land at the same time. Free-fall acceleration is the same for every mass.
