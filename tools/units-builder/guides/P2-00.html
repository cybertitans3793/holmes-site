Unit 0 · Greenhouse Effect (IB)

How to use this: read the key ideas, then cover each worked example and try it yourself before reading the solution. Do the self-check questions on paper before you open the answers, mark the ones you miss, and come back to them in a day or two without looking. Rereading won't do much; answering questions will.

Key ideas

  • This whole unit is IB content. AP Physics 2 doesn't test the greenhouse effect, though it does use black-body radiation in modern physics.
  • Every warm object radiates. A perfect emitter (a black body) radiates power P = σAT⁴, with T in kelvin. Twice the temperature means 16 times the power.
  • Wien's law: hotter objects peak at shorter wavelengths, λmaxT = 2.9 × 10⁻³ m·K. The Sun (about 5800 K) peaks in visible light; Earth (about 288 K) peaks in the infrared, near 10 µm.
  • Emissivity e compares a real surface with a black body at the same temperature: P = eσAT⁴, with e between 0 and 1.
  • Albedo α is the fraction of incoming radiation that is reflected or scattered back. Earth's is about 0.3, and it varies daily with clouds, and with ice, snow and latitude.
  • The solar constant S ≈ 1360 W/m² is the intensity of sunlight arriving at Earth. Earth intercepts it over a disk (πR²) but spreads it over a sphere (4πR²), so the mean incoming intensity is S/4.
  • Energy balance: at a steady temperature, power absorbed = power emitted: (1 − α)S/4 = eσT⁴.
  • With no atmosphere, that balance gives about 255 K (−18 °C). Earth's real average is about 288 K (15 °C). The difference is the greenhouse effect.
  • Greenhouse gases: water vapour (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), methane (CH₄) and nitrous oxide (N₂O). Each has natural and human sources.
  • They let most visible sunlight through, but absorb infrared from the surface, because IR matches the energy levels (resonant frequencies) of their molecular vibrations. They then re-emit IR in all directions, so some goes back down and warms the surface.
  • The enhanced greenhouse effect is the extra warming from human activity, mainly burning fossil fuels, which raises CO₂ levels.

Common mistakes

  • Using S instead of S/4 for the average sunlight on each square metre of Earth.
  • Putting °C into σT⁴ or Wien's law. Always use kelvin.
  • Forgetting the fourth power: 10% hotter means about 46% more power, not 10%.
  • Mixing up albedo (reflects incoming sunlight) and emissivity (how well a surface radiates).
  • Saying greenhouse gases “reflect heat” or “trap heat like a blanket.” They absorb infrared and re-emit it in all directions.
  • Blaming the ozone hole. Ozone loss lets in more ultraviolet; it isn't what causes the enhanced greenhouse effect.

How to solve an energy-balance problem

  1. Find the incoming intensity: S/4 for a whole planet averaged over day and night.
  2. Remove what's reflected: absorbed = (1 − α) × incoming.
  3. Write the outgoing power: eσT⁴ per square metre (e = 1 for a black body).
  4. Set absorbed = emitted for whatever you're balancing: the planet, the surface, or the atmosphere.
  5. Solve for T: T = (absorbed/eσ)1/4. Take the square root twice.
  6. Check: Earth-like answers should be a few hundred kelvin.

Surface and atmosphere

  • The surface gains sunlight and infrared sent back down from the atmosphere, and loses its own σT⁴.
  • The atmosphere gains infrared from the surface and radiates both up (to space) and down (to the surface).
  • Write one balance for each, and solve them together.

Ratio shortcuts

  • P ∝ AT⁴: compare by multiplying the area factor by the temperature factor to the fourth.
  • λmax ∝ 1/T: twice as hot peaks at half the wavelength.
  • T ∝ (absorbed)1/4: absorbing 16 times as much only doubles the temperature.
Wien's law

The Sun's and Earth's peak wavelengths

Sunlight peaks at about 500 nm. Estimate the Sun's surface temperature. At what wavelength does Earth's surface (288 K) radiate most?

Sun: T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ = 5800 K
Earth: λmax = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 288 = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ m (10 µm, infrared)

Incoming sunlight is mostly visible, which the atmosphere lets through. Outgoing radiation is infrared, which greenhouse gases absorb. That mismatch is the whole greenhouse effect.

Energy balance

Earth without an atmosphere

The solar constant is 1360 W/m² and Earth's albedo is 0.30. Treating Earth as a black body with no atmosphere, find its equilibrium temperature.

Absorbed per m²: (1 − 0.30)(1360/4) = 238 W/m²
Balance: σT⁴ = 238 → T⁴ = 238/(5.67 × 10⁻⁸) = 4.20 × 10⁹
T = 255 K (about −18 °C)

The real average is about 288 K. The 33 K difference is what the natural greenhouse effect adds.

Emissivity

Earth's effective emissivity

Earth's surface, at 288 K, radiates like a black body, but only 238 W/m² escapes to space on average. What effective emissivity does that give the Earth–atmosphere system?

Surface emits: σT⁴ = (5.67 × 10⁻⁸)(288)⁴ = 390 W/m²
e = 238/390 = 0.61

Seen from space, Earth radiates as if it were a black body with emissivity about 0.6. Adding greenhouse gases lowers that number, so the surface has to get warmer to send the same 238 W/m² out.

Surface balance

Back-radiation warms the surface

On average, Earth's surface absorbs 238 W/m² from the Sun and 150 W/m² of infrared from the atmosphere. If it radiates as a black body, what is its temperature?

Absorbed: 238 + 150 = 388 W/m²
σT⁴ = 388 → T⁴ = 6.84 × 10⁹ → T = 288 K

Without the 150 W/m² coming back down from greenhouse gases, the surface would settle near 255 K. More greenhouse gas means more back-radiation and a warmer surface.

Self-check

Answer first, then open.

  1. A black body's absolute temperature doubles. By what factor does the power it radiates change?
    Answer
    P ∝ T⁴, so 2⁴ = 16 times as much.
  2. Two stars have the same surface temperature, but one has twice the radius. Compare their power outputs.
    Answer
    P = σAT⁴, and A = 4πr² ∝ r². Twice the radius gives 4 times the area, so 4 times the power.
  3. Why is the average incoming sunlight on Earth S/4 and not S?
    Answer
    Earth catches sunlight over a disk of area πR², but that energy is spread over the whole spinning sphere, area 4πR². 4πR²/πR² = 4.
  4. Arctic sea ice melts and is replaced by dark ocean. How does that change Earth's albedo, and what happens next?
    Answer
    The albedo drops, because dark water reflects less than ice. More sunlight is absorbed, which warms the region and melts more ice: a positive feedback.
  5. Nitrogen and oxygen make up 99% of the air. Why aren't they greenhouse gases?
    Answer
    Their molecules don't absorb infrared: they have no vibrations at infrared energies that can absorb and re-emit it. Greenhouse gases like CO₂ and H₂O do.
  6. A light-bulb filament runs at 3000 K. At what wavelength does it emit most? What does that say about old filament bulbs?
    Answer
    λmax = 2.9 × 10⁻³/3000 = 9.7 × 10⁻⁷ m, which is infrared. Most of a filament bulb's output is infrared, not visible light, which is why they waste so much energy as heat.
  7. A surface at 300 K has emissivity 0.90. How much power does each square metre radiate?
    Answer
    P/A = eσT⁴ = (0.90)(5.67 × 10⁻⁸)(300)⁴ = 413 W/m².
  8. Mars receives sunlight of about 590 W/m² and has an albedo of 0.25. Estimate its equilibrium temperature as a black body with no greenhouse effect.
    Answer
    Absorbed: (0.75)(590/4) = 111 W/m². T⁴ = 111/(5.67 × 10⁻⁸) = 1.95 × 10⁹, so T ≈ 210 K.
  9. Explain, step by step, how adding CO₂ to the atmosphere warms the surface.
    Answer
    More CO₂ absorbs more of the surface's outgoing infrared. It re-emits that energy in all directions, so more comes back down to the surface. The surface now absorbs more than it emits and warms up until its own σT⁴ grows enough to restore the balance.
  10. What do the terms greenhouse effect and enhanced greenhouse effect each mean?
    Answer
    The greenhouse effect is the natural warming of the surface by gases that absorb and re-emit infrared; without it Earth would be about 33 K colder. The enhanced greenhouse effect is the extra warming caused by human activity adding more of those gases, mainly by burning fossil fuels.
