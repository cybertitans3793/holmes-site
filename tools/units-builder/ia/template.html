Use these headings, in this order

Following one clear structure makes it easy for the marker to find what each criterion asks for. There’s no cover page and no contents page.

Once your Stage 2 plan is approved, Make my IA doc in the planner (Stages tab) gives you a Google Doc with headings 3 and 5–8 already filled in from your plan. Make a copy, then add the other headings from this table yourself.

#HeadingWhat goes in it
1Report detailsFour lines at the top of page 1: title, your IB candidate code, group members’ codes (if any), word count.
2IntroductionAbout 150 words: what you investigated, why it matters or interests you, and a one-line overview of what you did. Be specific; skip “I am passionate about…”.
3Research questionOn its own line: how does [IV, with range] affect [DV] for [system], measured with […]?
4Theory and hypothesisOnly the physics that links your variables. The equation, and what you will plot to get a straight line. A prediction, ideally mathematical. Cite your sources.
5VariablesTable: IV, DV and controls, each with how it’s changed, measured or kept constant, and why.
6DiagramA labelled diagram clear enough to rebuild the setup from.
7ApparatusList with sizes, and each measuring instrument’s resolution and uncertainty.
8MethodNumbered steps, including how controls are kept constant. Be specific and skip the obvious. No calculations (they go in your analysis), but do include measuring anything you’ll need for them. Then safety, ethics and environment.
9Raw dataOnly what you measured, with the uncertainty in each heading and one sentence justifying it. Add your written observations from data collection.
10Processed dataA separate table of means, uncertainties and calculated quantities, then one sample calculation for each type.
11GraphThe linearised graph: title, labelled axes with units, error bars, best-fit line, steepest and shallowest lines, gradient ± uncertainty.
12ConclusionThe answer to your question, with numbers and uncertainties; the meaning of the gradient and intercept; comparison with theory or a published value; anomalies.
13EvaluationSpecific weaknesses and limitations with their relative impact; an improvement for each, and how it would change the outcome.
14ReferencesEvery source, in one consistent style (APA or Harvard), detailed enough for someone to find it.

Format rules

  • Font: Arial or Times New Roman, size 11. Spacing: single or larger. Text: justified.
  • Page numbers on every page.
  • Tables: text and numbers centred in the cells; every column heading has the quantity, unit and uncertainty.
  • Anonymous: no name and no school anywhere, only your candidate code.
  • No appendices. Anything that matters goes in the report; markers won’t look elsewhere.
  • Length: about 12 pages is typical.

The 3,000-word limit

Not counted: charts and diagrams, data tables, equations, formulas and calculations, citations and references, the bibliography, and headings. Everything else counts. State your word count honestly; it can be checked.
