Kepler's First Law: Drawing Ellipses

The orbit of every planet is an ellipse with the Sun at one of the foci. Draw four ellipses, measure them, and calculate their eccentricity.

Tools: Drag the ruler by its body. Use the Magnifier to read it to 0.1 cm.

Choose an ellipse

Procedure

  1. Drag the pencil all the way around the two pushpins. The string stays tight for you.
    0% drawn
  2. Take out the pushpins and label the holes F₁ and F₂.
  3. Use the ruler to draw a line from the outside of the ellipse through both foci.
  4. Measure the distance between the foci and the length of the major axis to the nearest tenth of a cm. Record them on your lab sheet.

Measuring tips

Drag the ruler by its body. Line its 0 mark up with the first point you are measuring from. Turn on the Magnifier and hover over the ruler to read it to the nearest 0.1 cm (1 mm).

String loop is 20 cm around. Based on the string-and-pushpin ellipse lab. Works with a mouse, trackpad, or touchscreen.
