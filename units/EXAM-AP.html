The AP Physics exam

AP Physics 1 and AP Physics 2 use the same format (May 2027). You can use a calculator and the equation sheet in both sections.

SectionWhatTimeScore
I. Multiple choice42 questions, answered in Bluebook85 min50 %
II. Free response4 questions, handwritten in a booklet95 min50 %

The four free-response types (one of each)

  • MR, Mathematical routines: derive relationships and calculate values, then make and justify a claim about the scenario.
  • TBR, Translation between representations: draw a representation of the scenario, derive equations and draw graphs for it, then show they agree or use them to make a prediction.
  • LAB, Experimental design and analysis: design a procedure, then plot and analyze data to answer a question.
  • QQT, Qualitative/quantitative translation: make and justify a claim, derive an equation, then connect the two.

Tip: the task verb tells you what earns the points. Check the Task verbs tab before you write free-response answers.

Task verbs

These are the verbs College Board uses in free-response questions. The verb tells you what earns the points.

VerbWhat it asks for
CalculateWork through the math to a final answer: the equation, numbers substituted correctly, and the answer with units and sensible significant figures.
CompareDescribe or explain how two (or more) things are similar and/or different.
DeriveStart from a fundamental law or relationship and carry out the math steps to the final answer.
DescribeGive the relevant characteristics of the thing asked about.
DetermineReach a decision or conclusion by reasoning, observation, or calculation.
DrawMake a diagram or schematic that shows relationships or physical objects, or that is consistent with another representation. Labels may or may not be needed.
EstimateRoughly work out a value, a comparison (greater than, equal to, less than), or a sign (+ or −) from data or experimental evidence. Steps do not have to be shown.
IndicateGive the information asked for, with no elaboration or explanation.
JustifyGive reasoning in words, beyond equations or derivations, that supports, qualifies, or defends a claim.
LabelAdd labels for units, scale, and/or parts of a diagram, graph, or model.
PlotPut data points on a graph using the given scale (or a scale you choose and mark, with units), consistent with the other representations.
RankPut quantities in order, usually by size.
SketchMake a graph, diagram, or model that shows a relationship or behavior, consistent with the other representations. Labels may or may not be needed.
VerifyShow that the conditions of a definition, law, or test are met, so it applies here; or use data, observations, or experiments to confirm or support a hypothesis.

Source: AP Physics 2 Course and Exam Description (College Board, 2024), “Task Verbs Used in Free-Response Questions”, p. 194, and Exam Information, pp. 189–193.
